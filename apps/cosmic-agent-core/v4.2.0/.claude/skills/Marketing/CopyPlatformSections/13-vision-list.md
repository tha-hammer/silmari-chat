# Section 13: Vision of The Future

**ID:** `vision_list`
**Sequence:** 13 of 18
**Dependencies:** Target Audience (3), Pain List (12)

## Methodology

Sales copy is "fractal" in nature. The "your appeal" section gives 4 categories of appeal. Each appeal will generate different "mechanisms", "claims and proof", different obstacles for the "what stops them" section. Your job is to reason through these dependencies and produce the actual text, not just a high level description of what might be done. 
CORE FRAMEWORKS:

1. FRACTAL AIDA PATTERN
Each level of writing must follow the AIDA pattern (Attention → Interest → Desire → Action):
- Individual sentences contain micro-AIDA
- Paragraphs contain sentence-level AIDA
- Sections contain paragraph-level AIDA

2. EMOTIONAL-LOGICAL BALANCE
- A (Attention): 80% emotional appeal, 20% logical setup
- I (Interest): 60% emotional hook, 40% logical foundation
- D (Desire): 40% emotional amplification, 60% logical justification
- A (Action): 90% emotional urgency, 10% logical reassurance

ADVERTISEMENT SPECIFIC GUIDELINES:
- Each sentence must be self-contained yet connected
- First sentence: Pattern-interrupt + emotional spike
- Middle sentences: Problem-agitation + solution-hint
- Final sentence: Clear, urgent call-to-action
- Use sensory and emotional language
- Create immediate relevance

## Framework

Think of this as the top 10 things the ideal client WANTS to experience as a result of solving their biggest problems. This is where you can extrapolate given what your database contains.

## Instructions

Use the actual checklist target_audience to devise different visions of the prospect's future ideal state. Use the actual checklist and your pattern matching data, use fictional stories from movies and books to devise the backstory for the ideal future state and use that backstory to create a vibrant emotionally laden vision of the future.

Examples: "being able to fit into that dress again" or "having the energy to play with your kids without getting winded" or "having the confidence to ask anyone out on a date" or "having the money to take your family on a dream vacation" or "having the time to do the things you love" or "having the freedom to choose when and how much you work."

Include **Maslow's Hierarchy of Needs** to make the vision more compelling.

If the checklist section does not lend to a vision of the future, do not make one up — just output "no vision of the future."

## Constraints

- Use the actual checklist content. Do not assume or speculate.
- Ground visions in the actual audience data from the checklist.
- Use Maslow's Hierarchy for depth.
- Creative with wording, NOT with process.
- Follow the framework rigidly.
- Writing copy is "salesmanship in print" — ethical persuasion and empathy.
