# Product & Brand Discovery + Competitor Analysis

**Purpose:** Understand where the product sits in the market, what the brand can
credibly claim, and how every meaningful competitor positions — so the offer can
be differentiated on purpose, not by accident.

> Going deep on sales message & customer here. We're touching the core
> foundations of the business: where the product fits in the marketplace and how
> to speak to the customer through the funnel.

Leave unknowns blank — they're discovery gaps, not fields to fabricate.

---

## 1. Brand Discovery

**Data & reporting**
- Do you have a reporting process for marketing? Describe it.
- Where do you pull/look at your data?
- What is your Customer Lifetime Value (CLV)?
- How much are you willing to pay to acquire a customer?
- What ROI on ads do you need to break even?
- What is your target (preferred) CPA?

**Website / product pages**
- Key URLs:
- Who is the product for specifically? What niche? (list 3)
- Where and how will this product be used? (list 5)

**General notes** — In general, what are you trying to accomplish? Who is the
clientele? Note distinct product ideas separately.

## 7. Enumerated Lists

Fill each list with as many real entries as apply (templates allow up to 10):

- **Unique Selling Propositions** (USP 1–10) — every message that makes the product compelling.
- **Target Customer Problems** (1–10) — problems customers have with products in this industry.
- **Solutions** (1–10) — what your product solves.
- **Benefits** (1–10) — what the product does for them.
- **Unique Experiences** (1–5) — experience the product offers.
- **Why** (1–5) — why a consumer should buy.
- **Negatives** (1–3) — honest downsides (used later to reframe / allay).
