# CRO Audit Checklist

Use this checklist to systematically evaluate each dimension of a page's conversion performance. Score each dimension, note specific issues, and cite behavioral evidence where available.

## 1. Value Proposition Clarity

**What to check:**
- Can a new visitor understand what the product does within 5 seconds?
- Is the headline benefit-driven (problem → solution) or feature-first?
- Does the subheadline add specificity or just repeat the headline in different words?
- Is there a clear "who this is for" signal?
- Does the above-fold content match the visitor's likely intent given the traffic source?

**Common failures:**
- Headline is a tagline, not a value prop ("The future of data" vs. a specific problem-and-solution statement)
- Subheadline introduces jargon without context
- Value prop assumes the visitor already knows what the product category is
- Multiple competing value props with no clear primary message

## 2. CTA Hierarchy

**What to check:**
- Is there exactly one primary CTA that's visually dominant?
- Does the primary CTA appear above the fold?
- Does the CTA copy describe the action and its outcome ("Start your free trial" not "Submit")?
- Are secondary CTAs visually subordinate (ghost buttons, text links)?
- Is there a CTA visible at every major scroll depth? (Don't overdo it, but don't make visitors scroll back up)
- Does the CTA match the visitor's likely stage? (Top-of-funnel visitors aren't ready for "Buy now")

**Common failures:**
- Two equally prominent CTAs competing for attention ("Start trial" and "Book a demo" side by side with equal weight)
- Primary CTA is below the fold
- CTA copy is generic ("Learn more," "Get started," "Submit")
- No CTA until the bottom of a long page
- CTA doesn't match traffic source intent (paid ad promises a demo, landing page leads with "Start trial")

## 3. Trust and Proof

**What to check:**
- Customer logos (recognizable, relevant to the target audience)
- Quantified results ("3x faster queries" backed by a case study, not just claimed)
- Testimonials with names, titles, and companies (not anonymous quotes)
- Third-party validation (awards, analyst mentions, open-source community signals)
- Social proof signals (GitHub stars, community size, adoption metrics)
- Security and compliance badges if relevant to the audience

**Common failures:**
- Logos present but no context (who are these companies? what did they achieve?)
- Testimonials are generic ("Great product!" — no specifics about the problem solved)
- No proof at all on a page asking for a high-commitment action (demo request, pricing inquiry)
- Proof is relevant to a different audience than the page targets
- Metrics without context ("10,000 users" — is that a lot for this category?)

## 4. Objection Handling

**What to check:**
- Does the page address the top 3-5 objections for this page type and audience?
- Are objection responses specific (data, architecture details, migration stories) or vague reassurances?
- Is there an FAQ section that covers real questions, not softball marketing prompts?
- For pricing pages: is there a clear path for "I need to talk to someone" visitors?
- For migration-heavy products: is the migration path addressed proactively?

**Common objections by page type:**
- *Homepage:* "What is this, exactly?" "Is it enterprise-ready?" "How is it different from X?"
- *Pricing:* "What happens when I outgrow this tier?" "Are there hidden costs?" "Can I try before I commit?"
- *Feature page:* "Does it actually work at my scale?" "How hard is the integration?"
- *Comparison page:* "Is this comparison fair?" "What are the tradeoffs you're not mentioning?"

**Common failures:**
- Objections acknowledged but not actually answered ("We take security seriously" with no details)
- FAQ is clearly written by marketing, not informed by actual customer questions
- Major objections completely ignored (the page pretends migration is effortless when it's a known pain point)

## 5. Scannability and Structure

**What to check:**
- Can a skimmer get the core message from headings alone?
- Are headings descriptive ("Ingest millions of rows per second") or vague ("Performance")?
- Is there a logical information hierarchy? (Problem → Solution → Proof → CTA)
- Are paragraphs short (2-4 sentences max for web)?
- Is there effective use of visual breaks (whitespace, icons, illustrations)?
- Does the page have a clear narrative arc, or does it feel like a random collection of sections?

**Common failures:**
- Wall of text with no visual breaks
- Headings are single-word labels ("Features," "Benefits," "Pricing") instead of persuasive statements
- Most important information is buried mid-page
- Page structure doesn't match how the visitor's decision process works
- Scroll depth data shows major drop-off before key content

## 6. Friction and Cognitive Load

**What to check:**
- Form fields: is every field necessary? Can any be deferred to later?
- Navigation: is the path from "interested" to "acting" obvious and short?
- Choices: is the visitor asked to make decisions they don't have enough context for?
- Language: is there jargon, acronyms, or assumed knowledge that could confuse a new visitor?
- Technical barriers: does the page work on mobile? Are load times acceptable?
- Dead ends: after reading a section, does the visitor know what to do next?

**Common failures:**
- Form asks for phone number, company size, and use case before the visitor has seen any value
- Page offers 5+ navigation paths with no clear recommended one
- Pricing tiers differ on dimensions the visitor can't evaluate yet
- Technical content assumes familiarity with the product category
- Mobile experience is a degraded afterthought
- Rage clicks on non-interactive elements (text that looks like a link, images that look clickable)
