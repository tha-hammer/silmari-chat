# Page Type Frameworks

Reference frameworks for evaluating conversion performance by page type. Each framework describes the page's job, what "good" looks like, and the most common conversion failures.

## Homepage

**The job:** Orient new visitors, build credibility, and route them to the right next step. The homepage serves multiple audiences simultaneously, so its primary challenge is clarity without oversimplification.

**What good looks like:**
- Above-fold hero clearly states who the product is for and what problem it solves
- One primary CTA with a clear secondary option (e.g., "Start free trial" primary, "See how it works" secondary)
- Social proof within the first scroll (logos, metrics, or a strong testimonial)
- Clear paths for different visitor types (developers, decision-makers, evaluators)
- Narrative arc from problem to solution to proof to action

**Common conversion failures:**
- Hero is a brand statement, not a value proposition
- Tries to say everything, communicates nothing
- No CTA above the fold
- Social proof is generic or absent
- Navigation overwhelms instead of guiding

**Key metrics to watch:** Scroll depth (do visitors get past the hero?), CTA click rate, bounce rate by traffic source, navigation click distribution.

## Product / Feature Page

**The job:** Convince an interested visitor that this specific capability solves their specific problem, and move them toward trial or demo.

**What good looks like:**
- Opens with the problem this feature solves, not the feature itself
- Shows the feature in context (architecture diagram, code snippet, dashboard screenshot)
- Includes at least one quantified proof point specific to this feature
- Addresses the "but does it work at my scale?" objection
- CTA matches the visitor's evaluation stage

**Common conversion failures:**
- Feature-first framing ("We support X") instead of problem-first ("When you need to X")
- No proof that the feature works in production
- Technical details without practical context (specs without "so what")
- CTA is "Start trial" when the visitor is still evaluating
- No connection to adjacent features or the broader platform story

**Key metrics to watch:** Time on page (are they reading or bouncing?), CTA click rate, scroll depth to proof section.

## Pricing Page

**The job:** Help the visitor choose a plan confidently. The pricing page is where evaluation becomes commitment, so clarity and trust are critical.

**What good looks like:**
- Tiers are clearly differentiated by use case, not just feature counts
- Recommended tier is visually highlighted
- Feature comparison table is scannable (not 50 rows of checkmarks)
- FAQ addresses real pricing objections (hidden costs, overage charges, scaling costs)
- Clear path for "I need to talk to someone" visitors
- Free tier or trial option reduces commitment anxiety

**Common conversion failures:**
- Tiers differ on dimensions the visitor can't evaluate yet (an internal-quota label means nothing to a new visitor)
- No recommended tier, forcing the visitor to figure out which one fits
- Feature comparison is exhaustive but not useful (lists every feature, highlights none)
- No FAQ or the FAQ is softball marketing questions
- Enterprise tier is a black box ("Contact us" with no indication of what to expect)
- Annual/monthly toggle creates confusion or suspicion

**Key metrics to watch:** Plan selection distribution, FAQ expansion clicks, "Contact sales" vs. self-serve ratio, bounce rate.

## Campaign Landing Page

**The job:** Convert a specific audience on a specific action. Landing pages are the most constrained page type: one audience, one goal, one CTA.

**What good looks like:**
- Headline directly continues the promise from the ad or email that drove the click
- One CTA, repeated at logical intervals
- Minimal navigation (or none) to prevent escape routes
- Proof is specific to the campaign audience
- Page length matches the commitment level (short for free resource, longer for demo request)

**Common conversion failures:**
- Message mismatch between ad/email and landing page (visitor expected one thing, got another)
- Full site navigation included, inviting visitors to wander away
- Multiple CTAs competing (sign up AND download AND watch a video)
- Generic proof not relevant to the campaign audience
- Form asks for too much too soon

**Key metrics to watch:** Conversion rate (primary metric), bounce rate, form abandonment rate, time to conversion.

## Blog Post

**The job:** Earn trust, demonstrate expertise, and create a natural path to product awareness. Blog posts are top-of-funnel content, so the conversion expectation is softer.

**What good looks like:**
- Content delivers genuine value (the reader learns something or changes their thinking)
- CTAs are contextually relevant to the post topic, not generic
- Internal links connect to related content and relevant product pages
- Author byline builds credibility
- "What to read next" section keeps the visitor on-site

**Common conversion failures:**
- Hard-sell CTA jammed into educational content ("Now sign up for [your product]!")
- No CTA at all (the reader finishes and has nowhere to go)
- Generic sidebar CTA that has nothing to do with the post topic
- No internal links to related content or product pages
- Content is thin or AI-generated and doesn't earn trust

**Key metrics to watch:** Scroll depth (did they read it?), internal link clicks, CTA click rate, time on page, return visits.

## Comparison Page

**The job:** Help an evaluator compare your product against alternatives fairly, building trust through transparency and winning on merits.

**What good looks like:**
- Comparison is structured and easy to scan (table format for feature comparison)
- Honest about tradeoffs (where the alternative is stronger, acknowledge it)
- Differentiators are specific and verifiable, not subjective claims
- Includes a migration path or switching cost analysis
- Proof comes from customers who switched, not just marketing claims

**Common conversion failures:**
- Obviously biased comparison that damages credibility
- Feature-by-feature comparison without context on what matters
- No acknowledgment of the alternative's strengths
- Comparison is out of date
- No social proof from customers who actually evaluated both options
- Doesn't address the "good enough" objection (why switch if the current solution works?)

**Key metrics to watch:** Scroll depth to comparison table, CTA click rate, bounce rate vs. other page types, referral source (are these visitors coming from competitive search queries?).
