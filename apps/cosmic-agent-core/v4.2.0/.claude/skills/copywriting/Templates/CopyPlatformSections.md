# Copy Platform Sections

> All 18 sections of the copy platform, with expansion guidance, per-section instructions, and constraints. Used by the CopyPlatform.md workflow during Phase 3: Expanding.

**Read before executing:** `CoreFrameworks.md` — every section below operates under the Fractal AIDA Pattern, Emotional-Logical Balance, and Advertisement Specific Guidelines defined in that file. Do not expand any section without reading CoreFrameworks.md first.

**Critical rule:** Every expansion must produce ACTUAL copy examples, not descriptions of what copy might look like. Fill in the content — don't comment on it.

**Dependency resolution:** Many sections are interdependent. Resolve circular dependencies by establishing the more foundational element first (typically USP → everything else), then revisiting dependent sections.

**Universal constraints (apply to every section):**
- Use the actual checklist content. Do not assume or speculate.
- Creative with wording, NOT with process.
- Follow each section's framework rigidly.
- Writing copy is "salesmanship in print" — ethical persuasion and empathy.
- Pain references serve as reminders of the prospect's actual condition to connect deeper, not as manipulation.

## Section 1: The Unique Selling Proposition (USP)

**Three aspects of a strong USP:**
1. **Uniqueness** — Must seem or be unique in the market
2. **Usefulness** — Must have a clear, specific benefit
3. **Conceptual simplicity** — Simple = memorable = compelling

**Construction formula:**
> "You know how [1-3 problems or dissatisfactions the prospect has]... Well, what I do is [how you solve it — no clichés, no jargon]."

**Instructions:** Use the checklist data to develop 3-5 USP drafts using the formula. Evaluate each against the three aspects. Refine the strongest. A USP is not just a list of statements or easily transferable to other offers — think step by step how to provide copy examples so the USP is presented in a persuasive manner.

**Constraints:**
- Use the "You know how... / Well, what I do is..." formula exactly.
- No clichés, industry jargon, or sales-pitch language.
- If unsure, ask the user — do not assume.

## Section 2: Claims & Proof Points

**Types of proof to include:**
- Results (specific outcomes, with numbers)
- Case studies (named or anonymized client stories)
- Social proof (testimonials, reviews, user counts)
- Mainstream press (media mentions, features)
- Living proof (personal experience, first-person account)
- Peer-reviewed studies (research, data)
- Historical proof (longevity, track record)
- Reason-why logical proof (the mechanism explanation)
- Story proof (narrative that demonstrates the claim)
- Specificity proof ("$1,347" not "thousands of dollars")

**Instructions:** Brainstorm all claims the user can make about their offer. For each claim, identify the strongest available proof type. Write specific, credible proof statements. Creative with wording, NOT with the process or the actual claim/proof itself.

**Constraints:**
- Every claim MUST have a matching proof from the checklist.
- Specificity beats generality (exact numbers, dates, names).
- No invented claims or proof.

## Section 3: Target Audience

**Initial checklist will have 1 audience segment. Expand to 5-7 segments using these questions:**

- What keeps them awake at night? What are they afraid of?
- Who are they angry at?
- What are their top 3 daily frustrations?
- Why is their current situation painful? How would they describe it?
- Why is their desired situation better? How would they describe the pleasure of arriving there?
- What's blocking them from achieving their desired situation on their own?
- How would they describe the thing that's blocking them?
- What lies are they believing? Why?
- What is their niche interested in that other people are not?

Also apply: demographics (age, income, occupation, location), psychographics (values, beliefs, fears, aspirations), behavior (buying history, current solutions, triggers), pain intensity, and awareness level (see StoryStructures.md).

**Instructions:** Expand the initial audience segment into 5-7 segments. For each segment, answer: Who are they? What do they desperately want? What do they fear? What have they already tried? What would make them say "this is for me"? You may need to imagine some answers based on the USP and claims/proof, but ground everything in the actual checklist data.

**Constraints:**
- Minimum: 5 expanded audience segments.
- Expand from existing audience data — don't invent audiences from nothing.
- Each expanded segment must be grounded in the checklist's USP and claims/proof.

## Section 4: The Mechanism

**The mechanism is WHY the solution works** — the proprietary method of executing upon the new opportunity. The biggest, easiest, fastest, safest, most predictable, and only way to capitalize on the new opportunity should be through the mechanism. It differentiates from generic solutions by explaining the unique method.

**Each mechanism should:**
- Confirm what the prospect already suspects is true about their problem
- Justify past failures (they failed because of X, not because of them)
- Position the mechanism as the missing piece

**Instructions:** Use claims and proof in combination with the USP to develop 12-15 mechanisms. Think step by step how to present each mechanism persuasively. You can be creative with how you justify the prospect's failures or confirm their suspicions, but NOT with the process of deriving the mechanism from checklist data.

**Constraints:**
- Minimum: 12-15 mechanisms.
- Each mechanism must trace back to specific claims/proof in the checklist.
- The mechanism is the "reason why" — never vague, always specific.

## Section 5: Why³ Framework

**The three questions ALL marketing must answer quickly:**
1. Why should I listen to you?
2. What's in it for me?
3. Why now?

**Blair Warren Formula** — apply to each Why³ level:
> "People will do anything for those who encourage their dreams, justify their failures, allay their fears, confirm their suspicions, and help them throw rocks at their enemies."

**Example copy applying the formula:**
- **Justify failures:** *"It's not your fault. The real reason you failed is because [external factor beyond their control]."*
- **Allay fears:** *"You're struggling to lose those last 15 pounds because you are literally fighting your own body."*
- **Confirm suspicions:** *"Yes, all the good salespeople ARE taken. But the best ones aren't looking for jobs."*
- **Throw rocks at enemies:** *"You don't need another doctor telling you 'it's all in your head' because it's not in your head."*

**Instructions:** For each major copy element (USP, promise, hook), answer all three Why³ questions using the Blair Warren formula. You can be creative with how you justify failures or confirm suspicions, but NOT with the process.

**Constraints:**
- All three questions MUST be answered for each major element.
- Apply at least one Blair Warren formula element per answer.
- Ground "justify failures" in external factors pulled from the checklist.

## Section 6: Core Appeal Elements

**The Four Main Categories:**

| Appeal Type | Core Emotion | Best For |
|------------|-------------|---------|
| **Sex Appeal** | Love, belonging, relationships, social acceptance | Personal development, dating, lifestyle |
| **Greed / Material Appeal** | Money, possessions, financial security, abundance | Finance, business, investing |
| **Fear / Safety Appeal** | Losing what you have, failing to gain what you want | Health, insurance, security |
| **Duty / Honor / Professionalism** | Serving others, high standards, status | High-ticket coaching, B2B |

Appeals are conveyed through emotions. Your goal is to make the prospect **FEEL something.** What do you want the reader to feel as they read your ad?

**Circular dependency note:** Appeal drives Hook, Hook drives Appeal. Resolve by: establish appeal first → develop hook from it → refine appeal based on hook.

**Instructions:** For the chosen primary appeal, develop 3-5 specific ways to express it in copy. Each variant should produce a different emotional register. Writing copy is "salesmanship in print" — not just a plain list of reasons or creative writing.

**Constraints:**
- Pick ONE primary appeal. Secondary appeals can support but must not dilute the primary.
- Every appeal variant must activate the Core Emotion specifically.
- The appeal must emerge from the checklist's target audience and problem — not arbitrarily chosen.

## Section 7: Features, Benefits & Costs

**The chain:**
1. List product features
2. Determine first-level benefits
3. Find the benefit of the benefit
4. Identify the cost of NOT having it
5. Determine the cost of that cost

**Example breakdown:**
- **Feature:** "We help you understand what is actually going on inside your body."
- **Benefit:** "So you stop taking things making you feel worse and start taking things to help you feel better."
- **Benefit of the Benefit:** "You'll spend less worrying."
- **Cost:** "Without this, no amount of pills or potions will work unless you just get lucky."
- **Cost of the Cost:** "You'll never have lasting results and will probably settle for feeling 'just okay.'"

**Wants vs. Needs:**
- **Wants:** Deep desires (transformation, recognition, freedom, love) — write to these
- **Needs:** Functional requirements — validate these

**Instructions:** List every feature of the offer. For each, write: the functional benefit, the emotional benefit (benefit of the benefit), the cost of not having it, and the cost of that cost. The benefits section should be 3-5x longer than the features section.

**Constraints:**
- Minimum: 5 features and 15 benefits.
- Each benefit must map to a specific feature in the checklist.
- Include the "cost of NOT having it" for every feature — not optional.

## Section 8: Core Promise

**A promise tells the prospect the BIG BENEFIT of consuming the marketing message** — NOT the big benefit of the product itself. The benefit of consuming the message is what makes them stay and read/watch/listen.

**A strong promise is:**
- **Specific** (measurable outcome)
- **Believable** (within the realm of possibility for this audience)
- **Compelling** (tied to a deep want or fear)
- **Time-bound** (when will they see results?)
- **Unique** (conveys your mechanism is different)

**Example promise:**
> *"Ask These 7 Simple Questions and Watch 50% of the Prospects Who Previously Told You 'I Need To Think About It'... Suddenly Pay in Full On The Very First Call!"*

**Formula:**
> "If you [consume this message], you will [specific result] in [timeframe], or [guarantee/consequence]."

**Instructions:** Draft 5 promise variations about the BIG BENEFIT of consuming the marketing message. Rate each on: specificity (1-5), believability (1-5), and desire (1-5). Use the highest-scoring version as primary.

**Constraints:**
- Minimum: 5 promises.
- **Promises are about consuming the MESSAGE, not the product.** This is the most common mistake — do not confuse the two.
- Every promise must be specific, time-bound, and tied to a deep want/fear from the checklist.

## Section 9: Hook Development

**A hook is a pattern interrupt** — it breaks the prospect's current train of thought and hijacks their attention.

**Hook Construction Elements:**
- **Call to audience:** *Did you know / If you / Are you / For ___*
- **Specificity of issue / circumstance / situation**
- **Revelation of a new idea, concept, or vehicle**

**Hook types:**
- **Question hook:** "What if you could [outcome] without [sacrifice]?"
- **Contrarian hook:** "Everything you've been told about [topic] is wrong."
- **Story hook:** "I was [in bad situation] when I realized [insight]."
- **Statistic hook:** "73% of [audience] fail at [thing]. Here's why."
- **Direct hook:** State the core benefit immediately, no buildup.

**Example dissection:** *"The 'Post-it Note' trick used by the world's top CEOs and high-achievers to do the work of 3 people with plenty of energy to spare"*
- "Post-it Note" is ubiquitous — the prospect can imagine it in use
- "world's top CEOs and high-achievers" is aspirational — the prospect wants to be like them
- "work of 3 people with plenty of energy to spare" is the specific, tangible outcome

**Circular dependency with Appeal:** The hook must express the appeal. Develop hooks that directly activate the chosen appeal emotions.

**Instructions:** Develop 10+ hook variations. For each, identify: the appeal it activates, the awareness level it's aimed at, and the emotion it opens with. Connect through meaningful, tangible elements the prospect can instantly visualize.

**Constraints:**
- Minimum: 10 hooks (5 if time-constrained, 10 is ideal).
- Each hook must activate the chosen Appeal from Section 6.
- Use the checklist's target audience language — don't invent generic hooks.

## Section 10: Headline Framework

**The 4 U's of Strong Headlines:**
1. **Ultra-Specific** — Specific claims outperform vague ones
2. **Unique** — Something they haven't heard before
3. **Useful** — Clear benefit or promise
4. **Urgent** — Reason to pay attention NOW

**Additional headline tests:**
- Does it seed a story?
- Does it spark curiosity?
- Does it drill home a primary benefit?
- Does it feel like breaking news?
- Does it imply ease or speed?

> *"If your prospect is aware of your product... your headline starts with your product. If he is not aware of your product but only the desire itself, your headline starts with the desire. If he is not yet aware of what he really seeks... your headline starts with that problem."* — Eugene Schwartz

**Instructions:** Write 20+ headlines. Minimum 5 per awareness level (see AIDAFramework.md). Mark each with its primary U (Ultra-specific, Unique, Useful, Urgent). Think step by step how each headline would be presented persuasively to the target audience.

**Constraints:**
- Minimum: 20 headlines.
- Each must pass at least one of the 4 U's tests explicitly.
- Must be grounded in checklist data — no generic templates with blanks.

## Section 11: The Big Four Elements

Enhancements that make any copy element more persuasive. Apply to every major claim, promise, and feature:

| Element | Question to Ask | Application |
|---------|----------------|-------------|
| **NEW** | What's new about this? | Lead with the innovation |
| **ONLY** | Is this the only way? | Claim exclusivity |
| **BIG** | How significant is the result? | Amplify scale |
| **FAST** | How quickly does it work? | Minimize time to result |
| **EASY** | How simple is it? | Remove friction |
| **SAFE** | What risk is removed? | Address fear |
| **PREDICTABLE** | Can they count on this? | Build certainty |
| **SIMPLE** | Can a child understand it? | Clarify |

**Instructions:** For each section of the checklist (USP, promise, mechanism, hook, etc.), apply the Big Four elements. Produce 3+ variations of each element applied to each key copy piece. Creative with wording, NOT with which elements to apply.

**Constraints:**
- Minimum: 3 variations per element per checklist section.
- Apply to EVERY major copy element — not a sampling.
- Each variation must stand alone as persuasive copy, not a fill-in-the-blank template.

## Section 12: Dimensionalized Pain Points

**Pain = the gap between current state and desired state.**

Writing about pain is not manipulation — it's empathy. The goal is to bring the prospect's awareness to their actual condition and connect on a deeper level.

**Trigger moments:** Find the specific incident that crystallizes the pain. Example: *"The moment she hopped on my lap and said 'mommy you're squishy.'"*

**Pain dimensions to explore:**
- Physical/tangible pain (what they experience every day)
- Emotional pain (how it makes them feel)
- Social pain (how it affects relationships and status)
- Financial pain (what it's costing them)
- Identity pain (who they're afraid they are)
- Time pain (how long they've been stuck)

**Instructions:** Develop 10+ dimensionalized pain hooks. For each, write: the trigger moment, the emotional state, and the copy hook that captures it. Use fictional stories from movies and books to expand and dimensionalize the pain. Write each pain hook as a question when possible — it creates immediate self-recognition.

**Constraints:**
- Minimum: 10 pain-based hooks.
- If the checklist section does not lend to a pain point, do not invent one — output "no pain points" instead.
- Pain is ethical — reminders of actual condition, not manipulation.
- Every pain hook must ground in the actual audience data from the checklist.

## Section 13: Vision of The Future

**The 10 aspirational outcomes** the prospect wants to experience after solving their problem.

**Maslow's Hierarchy applied:**
- **Physiological:** Basic needs met, no more daily struggle
- **Safety:** Security, stability, predictability
- **Belonging:** Connection, community, belonging
- **Esteem:** Recognition, status, pride, achievement
- **Self-actualization:** Purpose, growth, full potential

**Instructions:** Write 10+ specific, emotionally laden visions of the future. Each should paint a vivid, sensory picture — include the specific moment, who's there, how you feel, what you say to yourself. Use fictional analogies from movies and books to make them more compelling. Example: "Imagine being able to fit into that dress again" → expand to include the specific moment, who's there, how you feel.

**Constraints:**
- Minimum: 10 vision hooks.
- Use Maslow's Hierarchy — each vision should target a specific level.
- Ground visions in the actual audience data from the checklist (especially Section 3 Target Audience).
- If the checklist does not support a vision of the future, do not invent one — output "no vision of the future" instead.

## Section 14: USP Iteration 1 — Generate Problem/Solution Sets

**We are improving the USP from Section 1 through progressive iteration.**

**Instructions:**
1. **First:** Generate 5 different "Outline A Problem" questions for each audience segment (from Section 3). Incorporate pain points from the actual checklist. Start each with "You know how..."
2. **Then:** Generate 5 different "Outline Your Solution" solution sets for each audience segment. Each solution set should match an "Outline A Problem" set. Start each with "Well, what I do is..."
3. **Finally:** Combine the different problem/solution pairs into complete USPs stated as question-and-answer combinations.

**Constraints:**
- USPs MUST use the "You know how... / Well, what I do is..." formula.
- Minimum: 5 Problem/Solution sets per audience segment.
- DO NOT DEVIATE FROM THIS FRAMEWORK.

## Section 15: USP Iteration 2 — Evaluation Matrix

**Evaluate the USP variations from Section 14.**

**Evaluation criteria:**
1. **Uniqueness Test** — Must demonstrate unique elements from checklist
2. **Usefulness Test** — Must show clear benefits aligned with checklist proof points
3. **Conceptual Simplicity Test** — Must be easily understood without industry jargon
4. **Core USP Requirements** (from checklist):
   - Match target audience pain points
   - Include credibility elements
   - Support premium positioning
   - Include measurable outcomes
   - Address identified assumptions

**Scoring:** 1-5 scale where 1 = does not meet criterion, 5 = fully meets criterion.

**Instructions:** Create a matrix with column headings **"Criterion", "Score", and "Evidence from Checklist"** and one row per evaluation criterion. Score each USP variation. Total the score at the bottom of the matrix. Present the USPs with their evaluation matrix rather than asserting a "best."

**Constraints:**
- Scoring must be evidence-based from the checklist — not intuition.
- Present the matrix; do not assert a "best" USP at this stage.
- DO NOT DEVIATE FROM THIS FRAMEWORK.

## Section 18: USP Iteration 5 — Implementation Strategy

**Create a clear, actionable implementation strategy for each top-performing USP.**

Each strategy must show:
- **How** to implement
- **What** to implement
- **Why** to implement
- **How to connect** the implementation strategy to the checklist and the USP

**Instructions:** Use the actual checklist, the USP background, and the results from Sections 14-17. Develop specific implementation strategies for the top-performing USPs. This is the final step in the expansion phase — be concrete and actionable, not vague.

**Constraints:**
- Each strategy must connect back to specific checklist elements.
- Must be actionable — not vague recommendations like "use this in your ads."
- DO NOT DEVIATE FROM THIS FRAMEWORK.
- **This is the final step in the Expanding phase. After this, move to Phase 4: Implementation.**
