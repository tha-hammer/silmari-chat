# Emotional Triggers

> Catalog of primary and secondary emotional triggers for sales copy, with application guidance.

---

## Primary Emotional Triggers

These are the core drivers of buying decisions. At least one primary trigger should be present in every piece of sales copy.

| Emotion | Core Fear/Desire | Application in Copy |
|---------|-----------------|-------------------|
| **Fear** | Loss, danger, missing out | Paint the cost of inaction; make the threat vivid and real |
| **Greed** | More, abundance, gain | Amplify the reward; specificity makes greed credible |
| **Pride** | Status, achievement, recognition | Position the purchase as a status signal or achievement |
| **Exclusivity** | Belonging to the elite | Limited access, invitation-only, not for everyone |
| **Guilt** | Failure, letting others down | "Your family deserves better" — use ethically, with empathy |
| **Anger** | Injustice, being cheated | "You deserve better than what [enemy] gave you" |
| **Salvation** | Rescue from pain, redemption | The product as the way out of a dark situation |

---

## Secondary Emotional Triggers

Support and amplify the primary trigger. Use 2-3 secondary triggers alongside the primary.

| Emotion | Function | Typical Placement |
|---------|---------|-----------------|
| **Belonging** | "People like you do this" | Social proof, testimonials, community language |
| **Curiosity** | Open loops that compel reading | Headlines, subject lines, section transitions |
| **Indulgence** | Permission to enjoy, treat yourself | Desire section, vision of the future |
| **Urgency** | Act now, not later | Close, CTA, deadline framing |
| **Validation** | "You were right to suspect this" | Why³ framework, confirming suspicions |

## The 21-Emotion Dashboard

Map emotional beats through any story or copy piece. Each emotion should appear intentionally, not accidentally.

**Negative emotions (early in copy — create pain awareness):**
- Fear
- Anger
- Sadness
- Confusion
- Disgust
- Shame
- Guilt
- Envy
- Jealousy
- Loneliness
- Contempt

**Positive emotions (later in copy — offer the vision):**
- Joy
- Pride
- Love
- Hope
- Amusement
- Happiness
- Excitement
- Anticipation
- Surprise
- Delight

**Emotional arc for most sales copy:**
1. **Open:** Fear, frustration, confusion, loneliness (prospect feels seen)
2. **Middle:** Curiosity, hope, rising anticipation (the solution appears)
3. **Close:** Pride, excitement, delight, confidence (the transformation is real)

---

## Converting Emotion to Story Moments

**The formula:** "I felt [emotion] when I realized [change/new information] and so I [reaction/lesson]."

**Examples:**

| Emotion | Story Moment |
|---------|-------------|
| Fear | "I felt fear when I realized I had six months to change or lose everything — and so I stopped waiting for perfect conditions." |
| Pride | "I felt pride when I realized my clients were getting results I couldn't have predicted — and so I stopped doubting the method." |
| Anger | "I felt anger when I realized the industry was designed to keep me dependent — and so I built my own system." |
| Hope | "I felt hope when I met someone who had been exactly where I was and had found a way through — and so I reached out." |

## Emotional-Logical Balance

See `AIDAFramework.md` for full ratios. Quick reference:

| Stage | Emotion % | Logic % |
|-------|-----------|---------|
| Opening | 80% | 20% |
| Problem | 70% | 30% |
| Solution | 40% | 60% |
| Proof | 30% | 70% |
| Close | 80% | 20% |

**The principle:** People decide with emotion and justify with logic. The emotional arc must be complete before the logical argument can land.

## Blair Warren Formula

Apply to any copy element where you need to create emotional resonance:

> *"People will do anything for those who:*
> 1. *Encourage their dreams*
> 2. *Justify their failures*
> 3. *Allay their fears*
> 4. *Confirm their suspicions*
> 5. *Help them throw rocks at their enemies"*

Use this as a diagnostic: which of these 5 functions is your current copy performing? The strongest copy performs at least 3.
