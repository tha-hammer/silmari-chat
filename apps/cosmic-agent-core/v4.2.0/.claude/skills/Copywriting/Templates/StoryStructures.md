# Story Structures

> 9 proven story templates for sales copy, brand storytelling, and marketing narratives. Plus the 5T framework and the Hero archetype rules.

---

## The Prospect-as-Hero Rule

**Non-negotiable:** The prospect is ALWAYS the hero of the story. The vendor is ALWAYS the mentor/guide.

| Vendor's Role | Famous Example | Function |
|--------------|---------------|---------|
| Fairy Godmother | Cinderella | Enables the transformation |
| Mickey | Rocky | Trains and pushes the hero |
| Yoda | Luke Skywalker | Wisdom, guidance, belief |
| Gandalf | Frodo | Catalyst, opens the path |
| Alfred | Batman | Support, resources, loyalty |

Never position yourself as the hero. Position yourself as the one who makes the hero's journey possible.

## The 9 Story Structures

### 1. Rags to Riches
**Best for:** Origin stories, personal transformation journeys, before/after narratives

**Structure:**
- **Value:** The thing they want (and deserved all along)
- **Event ("no more"):** The moment they hit their limit
- **Struggle:** The journey and obstacles
- **Mountaintop:** The arrival and transformation

**Key emotion:** Hope and aspiration. The audience sees their own potential in you.

---

### 2. Fell in a Hole
**Best for:** Recovery stories, coming back from failure or rock bottom

**Structure:**
- **The hole:** The trap, situation, or circumstances they fell into
- **The fall:** How they ended up there (often through no fault of their own)
- **The bottom:** The lowest point — make this vivid and specific
- **Climb out:** The turning point and effort to recover
- **Other side:** The transformation — now wiser, stronger, and able to help others

**Key emotion:** Empathy and resilience. "They've been where I am — they made it out."

---

### 3. Nothing is Easy (Roller Coaster)
**Best for:** Resilience stories, entrepreneurial journeys, multi-obstacle narratives

**Structure:**
- **Problem:** Initial challenge
- **Early success:** Things start working
- **Setback:** First major failure
- **Bottom:** The darkest moment
- **Climb:** Recovery begins
- **Recovery:** Things start working again, differently
- **Better place:** Arrived at a stronger, wiser position

**Key emotion:** Grit and determination. Shows the audience that the path isn't straight but worth it.

---

### 4. How the Mighty Have Fallen
**Best for:** Humility and accountability stories, second-chance narratives

**Structure:**
- **Flawed power:** Position of power, but with a blind spot
- **Desperate denial:** Refusing to see the flaw
- **Deserved disgrace:** The fall

**Two angles:**
- **Defend angle:** The protagonist fights back and reclaims integrity
- **Exploit angle:** Show what could have been avoided — teach others from the fall

**Key emotion:** Humility and credibility. Vulnerability builds trust.

---

### 5. Epic Fail
**Best for:** Failure stories with clear lessons. Choose the variant that fits.

**6 variants:**

| Variant | Core Dynamic | Framing |
|---------|-------------|---------|
| **Catharsis** | Suffering and rebirth | "My fault — here's what I learned" |
| **Hubris** | Pride before the fall | "I put the cart before the horse" |
| **Betrayal** | Good vs. evil | "They weren't up to the task" |
| **Machine** | System failure | Impersonal causes, structural failure |
| **Zeitgeist** | Spirit of times | "We all did it — the culture was wrong" |
| **Nemesis** | Retribution | "They had it out for us" |

**Key emotion:** Varies by variant. Catharsis = empathy. Hubris = schadenfreude turning to lesson. Betrayal = righteous anger.

---

### 6. Risk-o-Meter
**Best for:** Tailoring stories to different buyer adoption stages

Match the story type to where the prospect sits on the adoption curve:

| Stage | Profile | Story Approach |
|-------|---------|---------------|
| **Innovators** | Risk-tolerant, early adopters | Lead with novelty, cutting-edge mechanism |
| **Pioneers** | Fast followers, see opportunity | Lead with competitive advantage, proof of concept |
| **Early Majority** | Pragmatic, need proof | Lead with case studies, results, risk reduction |
| **Late Majority** | Skeptical, need overwhelming proof | Lead with social proof, testimonials, mainstream acceptance |
| **Laggards** | Resistant, forced adopters | Lead with cost of not changing, peer pressure |

**Key question:** Where is my target prospect on this curve? That determines which story to tell.

---

### 7. The Odyssey
**Best for:** Journey stories, discovery narratives, personal growth arcs

**Structure:**
- **Home:** The starting point, comfort zone (may be lacking but familiar)
- **The need:** What forces them to leave (external pressure or internal call)
- **Unfamiliar situation:** Entering the unknown
- **Adaptation:** Learning, changing, encountering mentors and obstacles
- **Payoff:** The reward for making the journey
- **Return:** Coming back (changed)
- **Change living:** How the transformation affects everything

**Key emotion:** Adventure and growth. The audience goes on the journey vicariously.

---

### 8. Happily Ever After
**Best for:** Values-based stories, belonging and community narratives, homecoming stories

**Structure:**
- **Growing up:** The formative experiences that shaped the protagonist
- **Finding home/love/respect:** The belonging they were searching for
- **Doing the right thing:** The values-driven choice that defines them

**Key emotion:** Belonging and alignment. "These are my people."

---

### 9. Dragon and the City
**Best for:** Threat or opportunity stories, disruption narratives, market context stories

**Structure:**
- **The city:** The status quo — stable but complacent
- **The dragon:** The threat or opportunity appearing (could be external threat, or a new possibility)
- **Escape:** The initial response — run, hide, or ignore
- **Defense/Attack:** The choice: defend the old way or go on offense against the dragon

**Key emotion:** Urgency and stakes. "The world is changing — you need to choose."

## The 5T Story Framework

Use as the skeleton for any story:

| T | Element | Key Questions |
|---|---------|--------------|
| **Time** | Chronological anchor | "At first [problem], then we tried [action], and now we [result]" |
| **Turning Points** | Pivots in narrative | "We knew we messed up when..." / "The breakthrough came after..." |
| **Tension** | Peak conflict first | Start at maximum conflict, show resolution |
| **Temptation** | The ethical choice | When could you take the easy road but didn't? |
| **Teachable Moment** | The lesson | "What this means for you is..." / "The lesson here is..." |

---

## Cut to the Chase Principle

Every story must have:
1. **Action** — Open at the crisis or inciting incident (not the background setup)
2. **Emotion** — Maximum negative emotion early, maximum positive late
3. **Meaning** — Moral, lesson, or call to reflection at the end

**The rule:** If you can cut the first 3 sentences and the story still works, cut them.
